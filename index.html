1Executive summary

Problem

The current flow assigns a Driver before the order reaches the Merchant. If the Merchant cannot see when the Driver is expected to arrive, staff may delay preparation to avoid food sitting ready for too long. This response to pickup uncertainty can increase Driver wait and extend the Customer’s delivery time.

Recommendation

The product hypothesis is that making the preparation target visible and capturing readiness at the source will reduce avoidable Driver wait without shifting the cost to Customers through later deliveries, cancellations, or poor food quality. I recommend an event-driven MVP that combines a Merchant-level preparation target, order-level readiness actions, server-timestamped events, and the assigned Driver’s ETA as an informational signal. The Merchant App shows the preparation target/countdown alongside “Driver arrives in X min”; it does not calculate when the Merchant should start cooking. The Driver App shows the Merchant’s expected ready-by time and current readiness status for participating Merchants.

Keep the current Driver-assignment sequence in the MVP. Dispatch Engine changes sit outside the beFood product boundary and require a separate Platform decision. Use the pilot to validate the hypothesis before expanding scope or applying performance consequences.

Product principles and metric definitions
  • Merchant preparation time: ready_at - merchant_order_available_at.
  • Driver wait time: picked_up_at - driver_arrived_at.
  • Potential Merchant-attributable wait: compare the actual ready time with the later of Driver arrival and the applicable preparation target. Preserve the initial target and every approved revision; a late extension must not erase a breach against the original target. Treat this as a measurement candidate, not a penalty rule, until attribution is validated.
  • Use server timestamps as the source of truth. The preparation countdown is a client display derived from the server start time. Treat Driver ETA as a changing informational estimate, display its last-updated time, and measure its accuracy against actual arrival. Do not use it to calculate Merchant start instructions.
  • Keep the Customer’s estimated delivery time distinct from the Merchant’s preparation countdown.

2Product context and competitor references

The public beFood site provides Customer-facing discovery and ordering. Public beMerchant guidance documents order handling and a Merchant action to mark an order prepared. This proposal extends that workflow; Phase 1 must verify the current app behavior, event availability, and integration points before implementation.

Public competitor materials provide useful patterns for preparation-time management:

  • Uber Eats describes a Merchant default prep time, an estimate informed by the ordered items and order conditions, and order-level extensions.
  • DoorDash documents prep-time settings at item level.
  • ShopeeFood encourages Merchants to mark an order ready as soon as it is ready, even when the countdown has not ended.
  • GrabMerchant supports a Merchant “Ready” action.

These references support the proposed interaction patterns. They do not prove that competitors use the same dispatch sequence, incentives, or technical architecture as beFood; those details should not be inferred from public help content.

3Product decisions, assumptions, and scope

Terminology: Merchant is the restaurant partner; Driver is the delivery partner; Customer is the person placing the order. “Driver App” names the app surface, while “Driver” names its user. The experiment unit is the Merchant.

  1. Driver assignment happens before the order reaches the Merchant, as stated in the assignment; the dispatch sequence remains unchanged in the MVP.
  2. ETA availability assumption: the existing order/Platform API exposes the assigned Driver’s estimated arrival time at the Merchant, its last-updated timestamp, and reassignment state to the Merchant App. The MVP consumes this information; it does not change dispatch or Driver assignment logic. Confirm the API contract during implementation discovery.
  3. The Merchant App displays a fresh assigned Driver ETA alongside the Merchant preparation target/countdown. ETA is a reassurance and measurement signal, not a recommendation algorithm; it does not determine when the Merchant should begin cooking or reset the countdown. If ETA is missing or stale, show “ETA unavailable” and retain the preparation target/countdown.
  4. The preparation countdown starts at the server timestamp when the order becomes available in the Merchant App. The device renders the countdown from this timestamp; it does not own the clock.
  5. Phase 1 gives each Merchant one default preparation-time target. Per-item configuration is outside MVP scope.
  6. The Merchant can request additional preparation time on an active order.
  7. Driver features are released to all Drivers. Beta information appears only on orders from participating Merchants.
  8. Each eligible Merchant is assigned to a stable A/B group by Merchant ID. Every order inherits that assignment when it enters the experiment.
  9. The control group continues to use the current Merchant flow.
  10. Phase 1 includes countdown reminders, a lightweight on-time scorecard, a soft performance warning, and non-financial recognition. Phase 2 introduces the graduated operational consequences and appeal flow.
  11. Dispatch Engine changes remain owned by the Dispatch/Platform team and are outside the beFood MVP scope.

WS6 Phase 2 policy - graduated consequences

The policy design takes inspiration from food-delivery industry practices that use progressive warnings, temporary order-intake pauses, and campaign eligibility to address repeated Merchant preparation delays. Phase 1 releases the lightweight scorecard, reminders, soft warning, and non-financial badge. Phase 2 builds on that foundation with the following graduated operational consequences, using Phase 1 data to validate the metric and calibrate thresholds. Parcel-delivery late-shipment metrics are not equivalent to restaurant food-preparation time.

Measurement rule

  • Evaluate a rolling 7-day window. An eligible order must have a valid server-side order-available timestamp, the target accepted for that order, and a valid Merchant “Order ready” event. Exclude orders cancelled before preparation, system/API outages, and records with missing or conflicting timestamps.
  • Count an order as late when the Merchant marks it ready more than 5 minutes after the committed target. Allow at most one extension per order in the MVP, selected from predefined increments up to 15 minutes. An extension submitted before the existing target expires becomes the revised commitment; one submitted after expiry does not remove lateness already incurred. Retain the original and revised targets in the order history. Validate the 15-minute cap against Phase 1 data before activation.
  • Calculate late-preparation rate = qualifying late orders / eligible orders. Do not apply automated consequences until the Merchant has at least 20 eligible orders in the window. The Merchant target must also fall within the configured valid range; flag repeated target increases for review so a Merchant cannot avoid lateness by continually inflating the target. Check the proposed starting thresholds against Phase 1 historical/pilot distributions and fix them before Phase 2 activation.

Graduated consequences - proposed Phase 2 starting policy

Rolling 7-day late-preparation rate Merchant-facing action
Below 15% No penalty; show the rate and order history for awareness.
15% to below 25% In-app warning with the late orders, minutes late, preparation target, and practical guidance.
25% to below 35% Temporarily pause new order intake for 15 minutes; show the reason and allow the Merchant to resume once ready.
35% or higher Temporarily pause new order intake for 1 hour; show the affected order evidence and the next review time.
35% or higher in two consecutive 7-day windows 24-hour intake pause and temporary ineligibility for campaign/voucher participation, followed by re-evaluation.

Apply only the highest tier reached in a window; do not stack multiple pauses for the same evaluation. Do not apply a consequence when the event record is unreliable or a valid review is pending. False “Order ready” reports require corroborating Driver/support evidence and an in-app review before they can affect the rate or policy tier.

Reward for reliable preparation

In Phase 1, a Merchant with at least 20 eligible orders in the rolling 7-day window, at least 95% on-time preparation, no substantiated false-ready event, and cancellation/quality guardrails within the agreed baseline receives a Reliable Preparation badge in the Merchant App. In Phase 2, the same status can qualify the Merchant for relevant beFood campaign or voucher programs. Eligibility is not a promise of campaign placement. Defer cash rewards or fee discounts until the pilot demonstrates incremental benefit and the incentive cost is approved.

Fairness and appeal safeguards

  • Show the Merchant the rolling rate, denominator, each included/excluded order, target and extension history, readiness timestamp, and policy tier in the Merchant App.
  • Let the Merchant request a review from the order detail within 48 hours of a warning or consequence. Pause escalation on the disputed order until review is resolved; provide the decision and reason in-app.
  • Exclude late preparation caused by a beFood outage, invalid timestamps, or other verified non-Merchant causes. A Driver arriving early is not itself evidence of Merchant fault; assess the Merchant against the committed target.
  • Keep scorecard data, policy actions, and reviewer decisions in an auditable event history. Phase 1 uses the scorecard for feedback and non-financial recognition; Phase 2 releases operational restrictions after the thresholds and appeal process are configured and reviewed.

6Edge cases and logic

These are boundary, failure, and conflicting-state conditions that need explicit handling in the proposed workflow or its measurement. Routine operating variation-such as a busy Merchant or a complex order-is handled by the standard countdown and extension flow, not treated as an edge case.

Preparation target and countdown
ConditionExpected handling
The Merchant has no configured default, or the saved value is outside the allowed rangeApply the system default and prevent an invalid target from starting the countdown.
The Merchant changes its default while an order is activeThe active order keeps the target captured when it was sent, and only new orders use the new default.
The order is cancelled, rejected, or otherwise becomes ineligible before or during the countdownStop the timer and exclude it from preparation-performance measurement.
The app is backgrounded, closed, or offlineReconstruct the countdown from the server start timestamp. It must not pause or restart on the device.
Order-created, Merchant-received, and target-update events arrive late, more than once, or out of orderProcess each event idempotently and preserve the authoritative server timestamps.
Extension and readiness actions
ConditionExpected handling
Two Merchant users/devices request an extension at nearly the same timeAccept one update and show the latest saved target on all sessions.
An extension request is retried after a timeoutThe retry must not add the extension twice.
The order is already ready, cancelled, or completed when an extension request reaches the serverReject the update and show the current order state.
The Merchant marks the order ready while an extension request is in flightThe final state remains ready and the extension does not move the order back to preparing.
The Merchant taps “Order ready” more than once or a correction is requested after the actionPreserve one readiness event and use the defined correction flow without erasing the audit history.
Driver ETA as an informational signal
ConditionExpected handling
The assigned Driver ETA is missing or staleShow its last-updated time or “ETA unavailable.” Keep the Merchant target/countdown visible and do not block order handling.
Driver assignment or ETA changesRefresh the displayed ETA without changing the Merchant preparation target/countdown or suggesting when to start cooking.
The Driver arrives before or after the expected ready-by timeRecord the actual arrival and readiness events for analysis, without treating ETA variance alone as Merchant fault.
The Merchant accepts an extensionUpdate the expected ready-by time in the Driver App while retaining the original target and extension history.
Driver arrival and pickup timestamps
ConditionExpected handling
The Driver arrives before the target readiness timeRecord the early arrival separately and do not attribute the full wait to the Merchant.
The Driver arrives after the Merchant marks the order readySeparate Merchant preparation time from post-ready pickup delay.
The Driver is reassigned after arrivalRetain the original arrival and pickup history and identify which Driver performed each event.
GPS is unavailable/inaccurate, or the Driver fails to tap “Arrived”Mark the arrival timestamp as missing/unverified and exclude the order from automatic performance action.
“Picked up” is received before “Ready,” or Driver events are duplicated/out of sequenceReconcile against the authoritative order state and do not regress status.
No Driver is assigned or the assigned Driver cancelsKeep this separate from Merchant preparation performance.
Customer ETA and experiment assignment
ConditionExpected handling
Merchant extends preparation after the displayed Customer ETA has already passedRecalculate from the latest preparation and delivery estimates, then notify the Customer of the revised expected arrival.
ETA service does not return a refreshed estimateRetain the last valid ETA, show an update-in-progress state, and do not display the Merchant preparation countdown as a substitute ETA.
Multiple small Merchant extensions arrive close togetherConsolidate Customer notifications and show the latest estimate to avoid repeated conflicting updates.
A/B assignment changes while an order is activeThe order retains the Merchant’s assigned group captured when it entered the experiment.
Driver or Customer opens an order during partial rolloutShow Beta information only when that order belongs to a Group A Merchant. Absence of the new information on orders from Group B Merchants is expected.
Required timestamps are missing or unreliableKeep the order out of automatic reward/penalty decisions and retain it for data-quality review.

8Example implementation ticket - one sprint-sized slice

This ticket covers one independent Merchant App outcome: giving a Group A Merchant clear preparation timing and Driver ETA context. It assumes the existing order API exposes the preparation target, server-side order-available timestamp, assigned Driver ETA, ETA update time, and Merchant experiment group. If an input is missing, create a separate API/data-contract enabler instead of expanding this ticket.

Ticket: Show preparation countdown and Driver ETA on an active orderSprint-ready user story · Merchant App
User story

As a Merchant staff member, I want to see the preparation target/countdown and when the assigned Driver is expected to arrive, so I understand pickup timing without changing the preparation commitment.

Design
Wireframe for this story. Switch the state to see how each acceptance criterion (AC) appears. Numbers on the screen match the AC numbers.
    In scope
    • Show the Merchant’s preparation target and countdown on an active Group A order.
    • Show the assigned Driver ETA and its last-updated time.
    • Show Driver ETA as informational context only. Do not calculate a suggested preparation start time.
    • Show an ETA-unavailable state when the ETA is missing or beyond the configured freshness threshold. Keep the preparation target/countdown visible.
    • Show the Beta explanation to Group A Merchants. Keep the existing order screen for Group B.
    Dependencies / assumptions
    • The order API returns the fields listed above and an authoritative Merchant group assignment.
    • The Merchant default preparation duration is already configured or a platform default is available.
    • ETA freshness threshold is provided by the Platform/API contract; this ticket does not define or change Driver ETA generation.
    Acceptance criteria
    1. For an active Group A order, the screen shows the preparation target and remaining time derived from the server-side merchant_order_available_at timestamp; the device clock does not reset or pause the countdown.
    2. Reopening the order or returning from background shows the same remaining time implied by the server timestamp.
    3. When a fresh Driver ETA is available, the screen shows its estimated arrival time and last-updated time.
    4. The screen does not derive a prep start time from Driver ETA or change the countdown based on ETA.
    5. If ETA is missing or stale, the UI shows “Driver ETA unavailable,” preserves the preparation countdown, and does not block order handling.
    6. If the assigned Driver or ETA changes, refresh the displayed ETA without resetting the preparation countdown.
    7. Group A sees the Beta explanation and new information; Group B retains the existing order screen.
    8. When the countdown reaches zero, show the target as reached/overdue; do not mark the order ready automatically.
    Out of scope - split into follow-up tickets
    • Merchant marks an order ready and records the server-side ready event.
    • Merchant requests an extension and sees the revised target/countdown.
    • Retry, idempotency, and conflict handling for ready/extension updates.
    • Merchant scorecard, soft warning, and non-financial recognition (separate Phase 1 WS6 ticket); countdown reminders are a separate WS2 ticket.
    • Graduated operational consequences and appeal workflow (Phase 2 WS6).
    • Changes to Driver assignment, Dispatch Engine, or Customer ETA.
    Sizing note

    This is a bounded UI/data-consumption ticket and should fit within one sprint if the stated API fields and experiment assignment already exist. API work is a separate enabler if those preconditions are not met.

    10Roll-out plan and phase evaluation metrics

    Phase 1 - A/B pilot

    Primary outcome: Driver wait time at the Merchant location (median and p90), compared between Group A and Group B.

    ETA signal quality: measure ETA availability, freshness, and error at order receipt and before Driver arrival. These guard the reliability of the information shown to Merchants and support ETA-to-ready-by analysis; ETA does not drive a prep-start recommendation.

    Guardrails: cancellations, order completion, food-quality complaints, false-ready reports, Merchant opt-out/disputes, Customer ETA error, and event-data quality. Agree on the minimum detectable effect and guardrail thresholds using baseline data before launch, then keep those decision criteria fixed during evaluation.

    Release setup

    • Assign eligible Merchants automatically to stable groups through the feature-flag/experiment configuration.
    • Group A: Merchant target/countdown, Driver ETA context, reminders, scorecard, soft warning, and non-financial badge, with Beta UI.
    • Group B: existing Merchant flow.
    • Release the Driver UI to all Drivers. Show expected ready-by and readiness information only for orders from Group A Merchants.
    • Explain in Driver UI: “Some Merchants are testing preparation-time updates, so this information may not appear on every order.”
    • Keep the existing Customer App ETA experience during Phase 1. Record current ETA, actual delivery time, cancellations, and Customer contacts as the baseline for both Merchant groups.
    • Do not manually enable the feature for each Merchant.

    Evaluation metrics

    • Median and p90 Merchant preparation time (diagnostic metric).
    • Median and p90 Driver wait time at the Merchant location (primary outcome).
    • Gap between expected ready-by and actual Driver arrival; share of orders ready before or close to Driver arrival.
    • Share of orders exceeding the target preparation time.
    • Extension usage and repeat-extension rate.
    • Cancellation rate by reason.
    • Driver ETA availability, staleness, and arrival-time error.
    • Merchant views of Driver ETA, reminder engagement, and gap between expected ready-by and actual Driver arrival.
    • Missing/late/duplicate event rate.
    • Merchant and Driver complaints about early arrival, cold food, or inaccurate status.
    • Customer ETA error, late-delivery rate, cancellation rate, and contacts about delivery timing as baseline measures.

    Scale decision

    Compare outcomes on an intention-to-treat basis (by assigned group, whether or not a Merchant used the new actions). Expand Group A only when the primary outcome improves and pre-agreed guardrails remain within bounds. Exclude orders with missing or unreliable timestamps from performance attribution; retain them in data-quality reporting.

    Phase 2 - Customer ETA and incentive validation

    Release setup

    • Expand the Merchant feature in measured waves while retaining a randomized comparison cohort for the agreed measurement period. For the Customer ETA change, randomize eligible Group A orders into ETA treatment and holdout so the ETA effect can be measured separately from the Merchant workflow.
    • Keep Driver release global; display information according to the order’s Merchant participation.
    • Release the Customer App ETA update for orders from Group A Merchants. Group B orders keep the existing ETA experience. Show a Beta note on eligible Customer orders explaining that delivery-time updates are being tested with selected Merchants.
    • Keep the Phase 1 reminders, scorecard, soft warnings, and non-financial badge active. Build and release the graduated operational consequence tiers in Phase 2 after Phase 1 validates timestamp data and threshold calibration. Add Merchant-facing policy explanations, order-level evidence, notifications, and in-app review/appeal. Defer cash rewards until incremental value is proven.

    Evaluation metrics

    • Change in Driver wait time against the Phase 1 baseline and comparison cohort.
    • Change in on-time readiness and extension patterns.
    • Cancellation and order-completion rates.
    • Merchant adoption, satisfaction, and dispute rate.
    • Driver complaints and perceived wait-time predictability.
    • Customer ETA accuracy: difference between the latest displayed ETA and actual delivery time.
    • Rate of ETA changes after Merchant extensions; Customer cancellations and support contacts after an ETA change.
    • Share of orders delivered within the displayed ETA range.
    • Share of Merchants in each policy tier; warnings, pauses, review requests, upheld/reversed decisions, and repeat late-preparation rates.
    • Reward-policy cost and impact on sustained behavior.

    Scale decision

    Expand the Customer ETA update when displayed estimates are more accurate than the baseline, Customers receive the update before the old ETA passes, and ETA changes do not materially increase cancellations or support contacts. Continue or revise the Merchant policy only when improvement persists across Merchant types and time periods, consequences are not concentrated in data-quality failures, and appeal/quality guardrails remain within agreed thresholds.

    Phase 3 - Full release and ongoing optimization

    Release setup

    • Release the validated Merchant flow to all eligible Merchants.
    • Remove Beta messaging after rollout is complete.
    • Release the standard Driver experience with consistent readiness information.
    • Release the validated Customer App ETA experience to all eligible orders and remove Beta messaging after coverage is complete.
    • Introduce data-based preparation recommendations and any approved item-level settings.
    • Submit dispatch coordination as a separate Platform/Dispatch initiative.

    Evaluation metrics

    • Preparation-time prediction accuracy by Merchant and time of day.
    • Driver wait median/p90 and share of orders with avoidable wait.
    • Order cancellation, completion, and quality-complaint trends.
    • Merchant and Driver retention/engagement with the new workflow.
    • Incentive-policy effectiveness and dispute outcomes.
    • Customer ETA accuracy, on-time delivery against the displayed estimate, and Customer contacts about lateness.
